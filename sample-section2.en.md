# Section 2 — The three-step method: Find → Record → Match

> English translation of `sample-section2.md` (the Chinese version is authoritative; if the two ever differ, the Chinese one wins). Facts, roles and examples carried over unchanged.

This section is written for you — a community worker or volunteer coordinator. In one sentence: **stop waiting for older people to sign up, and start finding them, recording them and matching them.**

## 2.1 Step one: Find — not recruiting, but recognising

- **Start with people who are already moving.** In every neighbourhood there are people who have quietly been helping for years — fixing things, watching children, keeping someone company. They usually do not think of themselves as volunteers. They are your first resource.
- **Then look for the "silent capable ones."** Cooking, bookkeeping, writing and painting, repairing things, using a smartphone, knowing the law, knowing about care — all of it counts as a skill. It does not have to be "professional."
- **Ask through grids, buildings and interest groups.** A single question — "Who in this building has a real craft?" — usually works better than a recruitment notice.

## 2.2 Step two: Record — three fields are enough

| Field | What you write down | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Skill** | What this person can do (craft, experience, know-how) | Classifying the role (see the three paths in Section 3) |
| **Willingness** | Which kind of help this person wants to give | Matching — the older person chooses, you do not assign |
| **Availability** | Roughly how much time per week | Rostering and setting the length of a shift |

> The key: record **only what the older person says about themselves**. No scoring, no "capable / not capable" — the record exists for matching alone.

## 2.3 Step three: Match — three principles

1. **Build the list before you match.** Only once you have a record of "who has what" does "who helps whom" become possible.
2. **Let the older person choose.** Being turned down is easier to bear than being posted somewhere.
3. **Catch the people who come forward.** In Changsha, Mr Chen holds three certificates and still "never got the call" (see 3.2). That is not an older person failing to be proactive — it is a discovery mechanism that was never built.

**Quick-reference table: three paths (one page)**

| Path | Typical people | First move | Main sticking point | Sections |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Repair & craft | Older people with manual or repair skills | Find the capable ones already helping on their own; give them a status and a space | Free or paid | 3.1 / 4 |
| Volunteer service | No specific craft, but time and goodwill | Find first, then record; prioritise those who come forward | Being seen; staying | 3.2 / 4 |
| Teaching & transmission | Younger-old who have just learned something themselves | Keep the "teachers" that grow out of your classes; give them a status | Older-to-older vs. outsiders teaching | 3.3 / 4 |
