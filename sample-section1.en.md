# Section 1 — A change of frame: why an older person's experience is an asset

> English translation of `sample-section1.md` (the Chinese version is the authoritative text; if the two ever differ, the Chinese one wins). All facts, links, DOIs and PMIDs are carried over unchanged — nothing was added in translation.

## 1.1 Starting from two real people

**Liu Huiquan, 77 — Yisiyuan Community, Jiuxianqiao Subdistrict, Chaoyang District, Beijing.** A veteran craftsman who once ran his own repair workshop. Shortly after moving into the community in early 2024 he went straight to the residents' committee: *"I've just moved here. Back in Huoying I volunteered for my neighbours for three years. I still have the skills — I'd like to put them to use for people here."* The community worked with resident work units to free up a basement room of just over 30 m² in Building No. 17. He donated, free of charge, the lathes, milling machines, hundreds of tools and tens of thousands of parts left over from his workshop. On 5 March 2024 the "Craftsmanship in Service of the People" (匠心为民) service studio opened. From 9:00 to 17:00 every day he is there like it were a job — repairing furniture, doors and windows, sharpening knives and scissors — and he also runs free interest classes in paper-cutting, wood carving, gourd engraving and traditional mortise-and-tenon joinery. From offcuts of old furniture he built 40 sets of interlocking *dougong* (bracket-set) toys and gave them to children. (Sources: Jingbao Net / Beijing Daily app, 2026-06-11, reporter Chu Yingshuo, https://news.bjd.com.cn/2026/06/11/11799462.shtml ; see also Beijing Daily Net, 2026-04-22, https://peking.bjd.com.cn/content/s69e89291e4b0687a28946db9.html )

**Guo Jinrong, 69 — Yicuiyuan, Caohejing Subdistrict, Xuhui District, Shanghai.** A veteran whom neighbours call "Old Guo, he can fix anything." After enlisting in 1972 he was already repairing transistor radios for fellow soldiers; after retiring he picked the old craft back up and now repairs household appliances, sewing machines and bicycles for residents free of charge. When a small appliance needs a part he often pays for it out of his own pocket; for older and less mobile residents he makes house calls and checks back afterwards. When a resident wrote a letter of praise, he would not even accept a bunch of bananas — *"If you give me fruit, I won't fix that fan."* (Sources: Shanghai Xuhui official account, 2023-05-19, reporter Yao Limin, published via Shangguan News: https://sghexport.shobserver.com/html/toutiao/2023/05/19/1031499.html ; reposted by the Shanghai Municipal Veterans Affairs Bureau, 2023-07-06)

By the old lens these two are "retired people with time on their hands." By another frame they are **people who hold assets** — and the assets are not a bankbook. They are the skill in the hands, the judgement in the head, and the willingness to lend a hand. What you need to do is not "recruit" them, but **see them and catch them**.

## 1.2 Three frameworks: why "experience is an asset"

**① The successful ageing framework (Rowe & Kahn, 1997)**

In *The Gerontologist*, John Rowe and Robert Kahn argued that "successful ageing" is multidimensional: not merely the absence of disease, but the maintenance of high physical and cognitive function **and continued engagement in social and productive activities**. This was the first time scholarship wrote "older people still doing things, still producing" into the very definition of ageing well.
> Rowe, J. W., & Kahn, R. L. (1997). Successful aging. *The Gerontologist*, 37(4), 433–440. DOI: 10.1093/geront/37.4.433 (PMID: 9279031)

**② The active ageing policy framework (WHO, 2002)**

The World Health Organization's *Active Ageing: A Policy Framework* defines active ageing as "the process of optimizing opportunities for health, participation and security in order to enhance quality of life as people age," organised around three pillars: health, participation, security. The "participation" pillar is the basis on which countries build policies for older-adult volunteering and "Silver Age" action programmes.
> World Health Organization (2002). *Active Ageing: A Policy Framework*. Geneva: WHO. Document no. WHO/NMH/NPH/02.8 (policy document, no DOI)

**③ Five evidence pillars for "activation pathways" that have been tested**

- **ABCD asset mapping** (Kretzmann & McKnight, 1993): shift from "what is missing" to "what is here" — map older residents' skills first, then decide what to do with them. With the critical caveat from MacLeod & Emejulu (2014): beware of asset mapping turning into fine-sounding language for offloading responsibility.
- **Time banks (both successes and failures)**: positive mechanism samples such as Shanghai's "Huzhu Yanglao Shiguanghui", set against the "China Time Bank" scam named in a 2023 official warning (see Section 5).
- **The Experience Corps randomised controlled trial** (Baltimore, USA): an RCT in which 702 people aged 60+ took part in intergenerational tutoring (the design and rationale are described in Fried et al., 2013). **The published results are partially positive; one cannot claim measurable benefit across all health outcomes.** Participants' "generativity" (the self-perception of caring for and helping others) was significantly higher than in the control group, with a dose–response effect by hours served (Gruenewald et al., 2016); a nested objective physical-activity sub-trial (n=123) found that **women** in the intervention group significantly increased walking (about 1,500 steps/day), while men showed no difference (Varma et al., 2016).
- **Generativity theory** (Erikson): McAdams' (1992) Loyola Generativity Scale; Gruenewald's (2012) MIDUS longitudinal study.
- **Intergenerational reciprocity / reverse intergenerational support** (Frontiers, 2022): research on the relationship between support that Chinese older adults give to their adult children and their own wellbeing. (Note: the study is about *parents supporting adult children*, not *older people teaching younger people* — do not stretch the citation.)

## 1.3 One limitation that has to be stated plainly

Two of the frameworks above **come out of Western academic and policy contexts** (successful ageing from the United States; active ageing promoted by WHO). Their applicability to Chinese communities and the daily lives of Chinese older adults has not been rigorously validated on Chinese samples. So: **use the frameworks to help you look at the resources already in your hands from a different angle — never to stick a label of "successful" or "unsuccessful" on any older person.** Whether an older person takes part, and how, always rests on that person's own willingness and comfort. That premise does not change anywhere in this handbook.
